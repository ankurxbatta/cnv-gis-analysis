The City of North Vancouver houses 58,120 residents on 11.79 km² of land, giving an overall density of 4,930 persons per km² — among the highest in the province outside the City of Vancouver. It is overwhelmingly a multi-unit city: 85.8% of occupied private dwellings are apartments, row houses or duplexes, and only 10.6% are single-detached.

Density is concentrated sharply along the Lonsdale Avenue spine. Lower Lonsdale and Central Lonsdale together hold 61% of the city's population on a small fraction of its land, and the highest-density dissemination area reaches 25,960 persons per km². The same corridor dominates the public-space analysis: the twenty highest-scoring intersections are almost all on or adjacent to Lonsdale Avenue.

A notable validation emerged from the electoral analysis. The 2021 Census counted 41,130 Canadian citizens aged 18 and over in the city, while the City recorded 41,325 registered electors for the 2022 general local election — a difference of 0.5%. This is a useful consistency check on the citizenship-based proxy, though the two measures are not the same thing and are not presented as such.

3. Study area

The study area is the City of North Vancouver (BC ABMS LGL_ADMIN_AREA_ID = 114; Statistics Canada CSDUID 5915051, type CY). It is deliberately distinguished from the District of North Vancouver (5915046, type DM), the North Vancouver electoral districts, Metro Vancouver and the informal "North Shore".

Legal boundary versus land area. The BC ABMS legal boundary encloses 14.92 km² because it extends into Burrard Inlet. Statistics Canada reports the city's land area as 11.83 km², and the 79 constituent dissemination areas sum to 11.79 km². All densities in this report divide by land area, never the legal boundary area. Using the legal boundary would understate density by roughly 26%.

Principal sources

  • Statistics Canada — 2021 Census Profile 98-401-X2021006, British Columbia dissemination-area file (the BC-only comprehensive CSV, 293 MB, rather than the 2.2 GB national file), plus the 2021 DA, DB and CSD cartographic boundary files.
  • City of North Vancouver — the public ArcGIS REST server at gisext2.cnv.org, whose TransportMAP service alone exposes 230 layers covering roads, signals, parking, sidewalks, zoning and land use.
  • Province of BC — ABMS legal municipal boundaries via WFS; BC MoTI traffic count stations via the GeoServer WFS at maps.th.gov.bc.ca/geoV05/ows.
  • TransLink — GTFS static feed for transit stops, routes and frequency.
  • ICBC — Lower Mainland crash counts via the Tableau Public dashboard's own CSV export.

5. Data acquisition

scripts/01_download.py reads config/sources.yaml and downloads every source into data/raw/. Raw files are never overwritten unless --force is given. Each download receives a SHA256 hash and a .meta.json sidecar recording the source URL, licence, retrieval timestamp and record count. Downloads are validated so that an HTML error page cannot masquerade as a ZIP or CSV.

Two acquisition problems were encountered and resolved rather than worked around. The CNV road-classification layer contains eleven features the ArcGIS endpoint cannot serialise; the downloader bisects around them, retrieving 507 of 518 and recording the unreadable OBJECTIDs. Separately, the GEOweb download URL pattern initially assumed was wrong and was corrected against the page's own HTML before any run.

6. Data cleaning and validation

scripts/02_validate_sources.py re-hashes every raw file, verifies container integrity and confirms that vector payloads parse and carry a CRS. All 72 sources validate. The automated QA suite in tests/ then enforces CRS consistency, geometry validity, identifier uniqueness, population plausibility, spatial containment and the project's privacy and terminology constraints.

Validation that changed a result. The parking occupancy layer pairs an occupied-vehicle count with a published percentage for each surveyed period, but the field naming differs between weekday and weekend blocks. An initial pairing agreed with the City's published percentages for only 71–78% of segments. Investigation showed the pairing was in fact correct and the disagreement was one-vehicle rounding on very short segments, where Supply is a small integer estimate of a continuous capacity. With a tolerance of one vehicle on the segment's own supply, agreement is 99.8–100%. The City's published percentage is used as authoritative and the recomputed ratio is retained as a cross-check.

7. Census methodology

The BC dissemination-area Census Profile is a 3.6 GB long-format CSV. Rather than parse it in full, the pipeline uses Statistics Canada's own line-number index to seek directly to the 79 CNV dissemination areas, extracting them in about ten seconds. Every extracted row is verified against ALT_GEO_CODE to prove the index alignment is correct — an off-by-one at the block boundary was caught this way.

DA membership in the city is determined by representative-point containment within the CNV census subdivision polygon, because the cartographic DA file carries no CSDUID. The result reconciles exactly with the published CSD totals: population 58,120 against 58,120 published, and occupied private dwellings 27,293 against 27,293 published.

The 18+ proxy

The Census publishes ages 15–19 as a single band, so the 18–19 portion is apportioned as two-fifths of that band under a uniform-age assumption:

adult_population_18plus_proxy = population_2021 − age_0_14 − (3/5 × age_15_19)

A second and closer measure, canadian_citizens_18plus, is taken directly from the Census citizenship table. Because BC municipal elections require Canadian citizenship, this is materially closer to elector eligibility, and it lands within 0.5% of the official registered-elector count. Neither field is ever labelled "eligible voters"; an automated test fails the build if that term appears as a column anywhere in the outputs.

8. Housing methodology

Dwelling structure type comes from the Census at DA level and is complete. Building-level attributes are not: the City's 11,833 footprints carry only status and name, and only the separate high-rise layer publishes height, unit counts and year built. Coverage is therefore 0.9% for height, 0.7% for unit counts and 0.9% for year built. Buildings are classified from published evidence only, with the evidence recorded in classification_basis; 98.6% are honestly labelled UNKNOWN, which means the City publishes nothing about them, not that they are non-residential.

Condominium tenure is recorded only where the City's own Occupancy attribute contains STRATA. It is never inferred from building form or height.

9. Electoral and polling methodology

All electoral figures are aggregate and officially published. CNV runs "any voting place" elections: an elector may vote at any location in the city. There are therefore no polling-division catchments, and no polling-division boundary dataset exists in any format — a structural absence rather than a data gap that could be filled.

10–14. Transportation, traffic, transit, parking, safety

The road network comprises 941 centreline segments totalling 139 km. Intersections are derived by clustering centreline endpoints at a 5 m tolerance; a node qualifies when it joins two or more distinct street names or three or more segment ends. This yields 503 intersections, of which 91% lie within 25 m of an intersection in the independently published District of North Vancouver GEOweb intersection layer — a strong external validation.

Traffic. No AADT is published for any CNV municipal street. The BC Traffic Data Program covers provincial highways only, and its ten in-city stations are all on Highway 1 with counts no more recent than 2016. The City publishes directional volumes for just 38 of 941 segments. Only 40 of 503 intersections have a measured volume within the 150 m association distance, so traffic_score is primarily a road-hierarchy measure and is labelled as such.

Signals. The signal layer is a pole/asset inventory of 569 assets, not a list of intersections. Clustering at 40 m yields 133 signalised locations, of which 79 have at least one Full Signal and 54 are pedestrian-signal or special-crosswalk only. No cycle length or phase timing is published anywhere; the City releases it on request. Every signalised location carries signal_timing_status = REQUEST_REQUIRED and no timing value is estimated.

Transit. 172 TransLink stops lie inside the city, carrying 15,961 scheduled departures on a representative weekday across 15 routes.

Parking. 1,169 on-street segments were surveyed across eight time periods, with a total supply of 8,498 spaces. 502 segments (43%) reach 85% occupancy or more at some point. This is survey data collected by Bunt & Associates in December 2022 and January–February 2023, not a real-time feed.

Safety. ICBC publishes crash counts against location name strings with no coordinates, and its "NORTH VANCOUVER" value covers both the City and the District. Attribution required at least two named CNV streets forming a pair present in the CNV intersection layer, matching 288 intersections and 5,296 crashes. Intersections without a match are recorded as unknown, not zero, and receive no safety score.

15. GIS methods

All analysis is performed in EPSG:26910 (NAD83 / UTM zone 10N), the CNV server's native projected CRS, so areas and distances are metric throughout. EPSG:4326 is used only for web export; no area or distance is ever computed in degrees. Population within intersection buffers is areally interpolated from dissemination areas, which assumes uniform distribution within each DA — recorded on the output layer as an estimate, not a count.

The score is a neutral measure of how visible, accessible and feasible each intersection is as a public space. Seven components are computed as 0–100 percentile ranks and kept separate; the composite is their unweighted mean. Missing inputs stay missing rather than scoring as zero.

20. Privacy and ethical considerations

  • No individual-level data of any kind is collected, derived or stored. The smallest geography used is the dissemination area or block.
  • No voter identity, address, contact detail or political preference is collected. Only aggregate published election results are used.
  • Population aged 18+ is a demographic proxy and is never described as eligible or registered electors. The field name adult_population_18plus_proxy is enforced by an automated test, as is the absence of the term "eligible voters" from all outputs.
  • The public-space score contains no political variable — no party, candidate or voting history — and makes no inference from demographics to political preference. A test asserts that no such field exists.
  • Because CNV electors may vote at any location, votes at a voting place are never attributed to the residents living near it.
  • No dataset was obtained by bypassing authentication, CAPTCHAs, rate limits or robots restrictions. Where a source was unavailable by those means (CivicInfo BC's bot challenge, VCH's directory) it was recorded as unavailable rather than circumvented.

21. Reproducibility

The full pipeline runs with python run_pipeline.py --all, or stage by stage from scripts/01_download.py through scripts/20_generate_report.py. Raw downloads are cached and hash-verified, so re-runs are deterministic given stable upstream sources. Run python -m pytest before regenerating this report.
